This is my third year as a Printer Elf for IC3D's 3D Printed Toys for Tots program, and this year my goal was simple: be ready when the call came. I'd been stocking up on extra filament all year, especially whenever a sale came through, so when the program opened I wasn't scrambling for supplies.

Last year I made 111 toys on a single printer, my P1S. This year I added two A1s I picked up secondhand, and the H3C showed up late in the season. I set a personal goal of 600 and finished at 977, almost nine times last year's total.

Those are my numbers, and I'm only one of many Printer Elves. Nationally, we're up to 45,000 toys so far this year. Here's how my part of it went.

## How the program works

IC3D picks a set of approved toy designs (all donated by their designers). Volunteer "Printer Elves" print as many as they can, then drop them at a regional ElfHub, where they get counted and quality-checked. The hub hands everything to the local Toys for Tots chapter, and the Marines take it from there. I'm in the Tallahassee hub.

## What I noticed

I stuck mostly with animals this time, because that's what I was familiar with from last year. The turtles, dolphins, and hammerheads were the workhorses. The vehicles gave me problems last year, and I only managed one of each this time around.

I also got into multicolor this year, including on the P1S. It works, but it produces a lot more waste, so the H3C will be handling much of the multicolor from here on out. With that printer and more time next year, I'm hoping to deliver more vehicles and more multicolor prints.

As for the space they took up: I have a small workshop, and a corner of it has been full of boxes, one per toy, for a few months. Once everything was counted, it all went into three boxes. (Yes, the Amazon box really did say "Without you, I'm empty inside." Felt appropriate.)

![Three cardboard boxes stacked on a wood floor, one reading "Without you, I'm empty inside."](/images/uploads/toys-for-tots-2026-boxes.jpg)

Those boxes have been dropped off at the Tallahassee hub, and from there the toys go to the local Toys for Tots chapter.

## Why I do this

I'm really excited that there are 977 more toys out there for kids who might not get any this holiday season. I see the joy when I make toys for friends and family members, and I want to make sure every kid gets that same feeling. Each one of these toys is meant for a kid like that.

## Credit where it's due

None of this works without the designers who donated their models: Fixum Dude, Flexi Factory, Jennifer at The Dragons Den, Jopek Design, Javi Rodríguez (McGybeer), and Anselor. And thanks to IC3D for running the whole thing since 2018.

If this sounds like something you'd want to do, it's too late to print for this year. Signups typically start in June or July and run through around Thanksgiving. But you can still [sign up now](https://www.ic3dprinters.com/2026-toys/) to get added to the Discord and be notified when things kick off next year. You pick a local hub, and you don't need a farm. You just need to start. You can read more about the program on the [IC3D Toys for Tots page](https://www.ic3dprinters.com/3d-printed-toys-for-tots/).
